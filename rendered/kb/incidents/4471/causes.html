The three problems

parser: the shared parser reads day-first dates month first. Every tenant writes the day first, so a booking for 7 June becomes an invite for 6 July. This is the bug #4471 was opened for: the parser row. Tracked in furtivespy/what-it-shows#1.

hint: booking-v3 tells people to type the month first, then reads the day first. Its date field says MM/DD/YYYY; the form reads the tenant’s way. Only reports that came in through the form, from tenants on booking-v3. The date is wrong before the parser ever sees it, so the parser fix leaves these alone. This is the hint row, the one marked “not this ticket — no ticket of its own”. Tracked in furtivespy/what-it-shows#2.

One-day move: the invite lands one day off. Tuesday 12 May shows as Monday 11 May; a client in Lisbon gets Tuesday, a client in Toronto gets Monday. Swapping day and month cannot do that. The desk already read these apart (desk-notes.json): they are the footnote under the parser row, counted there by tenant. A footnote is not a ticket. The pattern points at invite dates crossing a time zone or midnight. Tracked in furtivespy/what-it-shows#3.

Lessons

  • Same subject is not same cause. Merge on cause: tenant, form and channel, then the body.
  • A footnote under someone else’s count is a ticket nobody opened.
